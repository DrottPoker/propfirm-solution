# Market data licensing rules and contract terms for a multi-tenant white-label simulated FX and metals platform (October 2026)

Research date: 2026-10-06. Scope: the rules and contract terms (not price lists) that decide what Ludware AB must license to show real-time spot FX (10 majors and crosses) plus XAUUSD and XAGUSD to traders of many prop firms (tenants) in a hosted, tenant-branded web terminal, fill simulated trades against those prices, and keep tick-derived candles and up to 30 days of chart history. Builds on section 2 of `research_notes/Konkurrenters priser och vårt pris/data_and_hosting_costs.md` (OTC, no exchange per-user fees, vendor contract is the gate, LBMA Gold Price needs an IBA licence, terms.law pitfalls) and does not repeat it.

Source conventions: "verbatim" = wording quoted from a document I read in full or in large part. Several vendor pages were read through a summarising fetch tool, so short quotes from those should be re-checked against the live page before they go into a contract discussion; this is noted where it applies. "Snippet" = wording seen only in a search result, not in the document itself. Sources older than 2025 are flagged "(pre-2025)". "Secondary" = not the issuer of the rule.

How we are described in this note (needed to apply the rules): Ludware is the only party that receives the vendor feed. Tenants get no feed or API; they get a hosted, branded terminal that Ludware runs and entitles. Ludware's own engine uses the prices to fill simulated orders, mark positions to market, compute P&L and drawdown, and enforce challenge rules. Bid/ask are widened per group (markup). Candles are built from ticks and stored; up to 30 days of chart history are kept and re-served.

## 1. Display vs non-display use: how is non-display defined, and does a simulated fill engine or rule engine count?

### Takeaway
Under exchange and venue policies (Cboe 2026, ICE 2026, CME/EBS), anything a machine does with prices that is not purely to put them on a screen is non-display use, and the listed examples (order processing, P&L, risk management, trade internalisation, automated trading) describe our fill and rule engines almost exactly, so with venue data these would be separately licensed and fee-liable. The OTC API vendors we are likely to use (Twelve Data, Tiingo, TraderMade, Live-Rates) do not publish a separate non-display fee; there the engine is normally covered as "internal use", but because the engine's outputs (fill prices, P&L) are shown to third parties, the real issue becomes the external display/redistribution grant, and the permission to run a simulated execution engine for end users should be written into the licence.

### Cited Findings
- Cboe (North American data policies, updated 2026): "Non-Display Usage is defined as any method of accessing, or facilitating access to, a Market Data product that involves access or use by a machine or automated device for a purpose that is not solely in support of display for a natural person or persons." (snippet). The change was explained as capturing firms "leveraging Large Language Models" - [Cboe SR-CboeBZX-2026-033 filing](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/regulation/rule_filings/approved/2026/SR-CboeBZX-2026-033.pdf). A Federal Register summary dated 2026-04-28 gives the effective date as 2026-04-21 and paraphrases the definition as any machine access "that is not solely for display to a person", naming "feeding data into large language models (LLMs) or other 'black box' solutions" - [PolicyRisk summary of FR 2026-08186](https://policyrisk.com/federal-register/2026-08186). Conflict: the search snippet said effective 2026-04-01; the FR summary says 2026-04-21. Note this is Cboe's equities/options policy; no Cboe FX (spot ECN) data policy text was found.
- ICE Futures Europe Market Data Policy, Version 4, January 2026 (verbatim): "Datafeed or non-display, defined as accessing, processing or consuming the Exchange's market data for a purpose other than in support of the user's display or further internal / external redistribution." Unit of count: "For display data, the Exchange uses entitlement per active user as the unit of count, and for non-display data it uses per device as the unit of count." - [ICE Futures Europe Market Data Policy v4 (Jan 2026)](https://www.ice.com/publicdocs/futures/ICE_Futures_Europe_Market_Data_Policy.pdf)
- CME Group / EBS (pre-2025, snippet): "Non-Display Use is non-viewable use of Information, internally within a Licensee's Group, by/in any system, process, program, machine or calculation other than in order to display or distribute Information for display use. Such use may include, but is not limited to, calculation of P&L, portfolio valuation/management, order processing, risk management, trade internalization, research & analysis, use within Automated/Semi-Automated/Programmatic/Electronic Trading Systems and automated order routing." - [CME EBS MiFID II market data overview, Sept 2024](https://cmegroup.com/trading/market-tech-and-data-services/files/mifidii-market-data-overview-ebs-sept-2024.pdf) (the PDF timed out three times; wording is from the search index). CME splits non-display into categories, with "Category A - Automated Trading Usage" and Category C sub-categories "C1 Risk Management, C2 Research and Analysis, C3 Fund Administration, C4 Portfolio Management and C5 Other" (snippets) - [CME non-display licensing FAQ, Oct 2024](https://cmegroup.com/market-data/distributor/files/cme-group-data-licensing-policy-guidelines-and-non-display-licensing-faq-october-2024.pdf); [CME fee list Jan 2026](https://www.cmegroup.com/market-data/files/january-2026-market-data-fee-list.pdf)
- Cboe BZX fee filing (pre-2025, 2018, equities) defines a hosted non-display category that matches a platform operator: "A Managed Non-Display Service Provider is a Distributor that host a subscriber's applications that utilizes a Market Data Product within the Managed Non-Display Service Provider's space/cage; fully manages and controls access to the Market Data Product, and does not permit further redistribution of the Market Data Product internally or externally." (verbatim) - [SR-CboeBZX-2018-065, Exhibit 5](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/cboebzx/2018/34-84002-ex5.pdf)

The FISD documents themselves were not read.
- ESMA's standard terminology (as quoted in the ICE policy, Jan 2026): "Display Data should mean the market data provided or used through the support of a monitor or a screen and that is human readable." "Non-Display Data should mean all the market data which does not meet the definition of Display Data." - [ICE Futures Europe Market Data Policy v4](https://www.ice.com/publicdocs/futures/ICE_Futures_Europe_Market_Data_Policy.pdf), citing [ESMA70-156-4263](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/library/esma70-156-4263_guidelines_mifid_ii_mifir_obligations_on_market_data.pdf)
- Twelve Data Terms of Use (read via summarising fetch; fetched page says "Last Updated: January 1, 2026"): "Non-Display Use": "any use of Data that does not involve displaying the Data to natural persons"; "Authorized User": "all of Customer's employees, contractors, and computerized systems expressly authorized by Customer to use the Platform"; base grant is "solely for Internal Use", meaning "use solely for Customer's internal business purposes and not for redistribution or external commercial purposes"; "Professional Use": "use by entities required to pay professional subscriber rates under exchange rules" - [Twelve Data Terms](https://twelvedata.com/terms). Conflict: the search index title of the same URL reads "Last updated: October 24, 2020"; the fetched page shows 2026-01-01. Twelve Data's published price list has no separate non-display line (prior note) - [Twelve Data business pricing](https://twelvedata.com/pricing-business)
- Tiingo Terms of Use (verbatim, "Last Updated Date: August 5th, 2026"): "All data via the API is for internal consumption only ... Redistribution is only available upon special request and permission, and comes with additional fees." No non-display concept appears - [Tiingo Terms of Use](https://app.tiingo.com/tos/)
- TraderMade terms (read via summarising fetch, undated): "Fair Business Use" means rates "for internal purposes or internal applications"; external display needs prior permission - [TraderMade terms](https://marketdata.tradermade.com/termsandconditions)
- Live-Rates Terms of Service (verbatim, "Last updated: October 2026"): with an active paid plan you may "Use the data received through the API in your own applications, websites, internal tools and systems" - [Live-Rates terms](https://www.live-rates.com/terms-privacy)
- Fee mechanics (secondary): "Non-display fees are paid to an exchange for the use of market data for non-display use, including trading applications like execution algorithms, smart order routing (SORs) or market making" - [Markets Media: market data fees, an overview](https://www.marketsmedia.com/market-data-fees-an-overview)

### Inferences
- Our simulated matching engine (fill at bid/ask), stop-loss/take-profit triggering, margin and equity calculation, drawdown and daily-loss rule checks, and any automatic account breach are non-display use under every exchange-style definition above. The CME list ("calculation of P&L ... order processing, risk management, trade internalization") reads like a description of our engine. It does not matter that the trades are simulated; the definitions look at what the machine does with the data, not whether money moves. No policy found exempts simulated or paper trading.
- With a venue feed (EBS, Cboe FX, LMAX, a CME product) we should expect a non-display licence on top of display, counted per device/application/server (ICE: per device), and possibly a hosted category like Cboe's "Managed Non-Display Service Provider", because our engine works on behalf of other firms' traders. CME's definition limits non-display to use "internally within a Licensee's Group", which our engine (serving tenants' traders) does not fit; that points to a platform/distributor category, not an internal one. This is an inference; no FX venue non-display schedule was read.
- With OTC API vendors, non-display is normally inside the base "internal use" grant, so it is not a separate fee. But the engine's outputs are external: a fill at 1.08523 shows the trader the (marked-up) bid at that moment, and P&L is computed from it. So the licence we buy must be an external display/redistribution licence that also explicitly permits "use in a simulated execution, matching and rule engine operated for end users of our customers". If the contract is silent, a vendor could argue that "internal applications" do not include an engine that serves third parties.
- Twelve Data's "Professional Use" term ties pro status to exchange rules. Spot FX has no exchange pro/non-pro regime, so the term should not bite, but we should get written confirmation that no professional/non-professional classification applies to our end users (prop traders trading firm-branded simulated capital could otherwise be argued to be "professional").

### Gaps
- No Cboe FX (spot ECN), LMAX, EBS spot, LSEG Matching or Bloomberg FX non-display policy text was found; LMAX publishes institutional FX data via the Pyth network but its licence terms were not found - [Pyth: conversation with LMAX](https://www.pyth.network/blog/a-new-model-for-market-data-distribution-an-executive-conversation-with-lmax).
- The CME documents could not be opened (timeouts); definitions rely on search snippets.
- No vendor or venue policy found that mentions simulated trading, paper trading, demo accounts or prop challenges by name.

## 2. Redistribution and white label: what is showing data to other companies' end users called, and must each tenant sign or be reported?

### Takeaway
Under the vendor terms we would sign, our tenants' traders are "third parties", so showing them prices is "external display" or "redistribution", and putting it under each prop firm's brand is "white labeling", which Twelve Data sells only on its top tier or by separate agreement and exchanges price per white-label platform. Exchange regimes commonly make each downstream firm sign or be declared (ICE: a Derived Data Agreement with each client; monthly declaration of all end users; Cboe: fee per white-label platform), while the published API vendor terms say nothing about per-tenant agreements, so this must be settled in writing.

### Cited Findings
- White-label definition (pre-2025, Cboe BZX, 2018, verbatim): "A 'White Label Service' is a type of hosted display solution in which a Distributor hosts or maintains a website or platform on behalf of a third-party entity. The service allows Distributors to make Derived Data available on a platform that is branded with a third-party brand, or co-branded with a third party and a Distributor. The Distributor maintains control of the application's data, entitlements and display." - [SR-CboeBZX-2018-065, Exhibit 5](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/cboebzx/2018/34-84002-ex5.pdf)
- Same filing, fees counted per tenant: the White Label program charged USD 300/200 per month "per External Subscriber" and "The External Subscriber Fee is non-progressive and is based on the number of White Label Service Platforms (i.e., 'External Subscribers') that receive Derived Data from the Distributor. For example, a Distributor providing Derived Data based on BZX Top to six External Subscribers would be charged a monthly fee of $1,500 (i.e., 6 External Subscribers x $250 each)." Also: "A Distributor that provides a White Label Service for BZX Top data that is not Derived Data ... would be liable for the fees normally applicable for the distribution of BZX Top." (verbatim) - [SR-CboeBZX-2018-065, Exhibit 5](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/cboebzx/2018/34-84002-ex5.pdf)
- ICE (Jan 2026, verbatim): "Users who wish to redistribute the Exchange's data must execute a Quote Vendor Agreement"; "Authorized Vendors are subject to monthly reporting obligations"; for derived data: "Before distributing Derived Data to a Client, the Licensee must inter alia execute a Derived Data Agreement with the Client. ICE Data reserves the right to refuse access to or revoke access granted to any Client." Index licensees may provide products to end-user clients only if they "have executed an end user license agreement" - [ICE Futures Europe Market Data Policy v4](https://www.ice.com/publicdocs/futures/ICE_Futures_Europe_Market_Data_Policy.pdf)
- Twelve Data Terms (summarising fetch, 2026-01-01): "Redistribution": "any publication, distribution, or provision of Data to third parties"; customer may "Redistribute or provide external display of Data only if and as expressly authorized by a Redistribution Rights Add-On or separate written agreement"; customer shall not "Sublicense ... Data or access rights to third parties except as expressly permitted"; Section 18.2 white-label options "Subject to separate agreement" include "Redistribution rights", "Co-branding opportunities" and "Custom attribution requirements" - [Twelve Data Terms](https://twelvedata.com/terms). Price list tiers: Venture "External display data access", Enterprise "External distribution market data", Enterprise+ (custom) "White-labeling data access" (prior note) - [Twelve Data business pricing](https://twelvedata.com/pricing-business)
- Twelve Data attribution guide (verbatim, 2026-01-12): attribution is required when "Content is redistributed or embedded into client-facing products"; not required for "White-label solutions purchased directly from Twelve Data, where the license explicitly includes attribution rights"; redistributors must "Require your downstream users to maintain attribution when displaying the data publicly"; "Failure to attribute properly may lead to suspension or termination of your data access" - [Twelve Data attribution guidelines](https://support.twelvedata.com/en/articles/12647398-attribution-guidelines-for-using-twelve-data)
- Tiingo (verbatim, 2026-08-05): "In the event that Tiingo permits you to redistribute any data ... you must include with any redistribution, the phrase 'Data sourced by Tiingo' with a link to https://www.tiingo.com. Users may use a different form of attribution, but only upon the prior, written approval of Tiingo." Also the general restriction that you "shall not license, sell, rent, lease, transfer, assign, reproduce, distribute, host or otherwise commercially exploit Company Properties" - [Tiingo Terms of Use](https://app.tiingo.com/tos/)
- TraderMade (summarising fetch): users cannot "disseminate, distribute, publish, display or otherwise disclose" data without authorisation and may not "sublicense"; Enterprise plan lists "white-labeling rights" (prior note) - [TraderMade terms](https://marketdata.tradermade.com/termsandconditions); [TraderMade pricing](https://tradermade.com/pricing)
- Live-Rates (verbatim, Oct 2026): paid plans allow you to "Display the data to your own end users, either as received or derived from it (for example charts, OHLC candles, currency conversions, widgets, alerts and analytics)" but you may not "Resell, relay or sublicense the data, raw or lightly transformed, to third parties as a data feed or API" - [Live-Rates terms](https://www.live-rates.com/terms-privacy)
- General principle (secondary): "Internal redistribution covers sharing within a legal entity or defined corporate group, while external redistribution covers sharing with clients, counterparties, or the public" - [Elliptic corpus: market data licensing](https://www.elliptic.co/corpus/gen-3835/market-data/market-data-licensing-redistribution-rights-and-compliance-controls.html)

### Inferences
- Classification of our model: Ludware is the licensee and "Distributor"; tenants are third-party entities on whose behalf we host a branded platform; tenant traders are end users of a third party. That is the Cboe "White Label Service" pattern word for word ("hosts or maintains a website or platform on behalf of a third-party entity ... branded with a third-party brand ... Distributor maintains control of the application's data, entitlements and display"). In Twelve Data's terms it is "external display" at minimum and "white-labeling" when the vendor's name is not shown; Venture ("External display") may not be enough, Enterprise+ is the tier that names white labeling.
- It is not "sub-licensing" as long as tenants receive no feed, no API key and no right to pass data on, and Ludware keeps control of entitlements. It becomes sub-licensing or "relay as a data feed" if tenants (or their traders) can pull a raw stream from our WebSocket for their own systems, or if a tenant's own site embeds our prices. Our tenant contract should forbid this and our API should be authenticated per trader, rate-limited and not documented as a public feed.
- Live-Rates' "your own end users" is the key ambiguity for a white-label model: tenant traders are not Ludware's own customers in a contractual sense (they contract with the tenant). Ask for written confirmation that end users of our customers' branded instances count as "your own end users".
- Attribution clashes with white label: Tiingo and Twelve Data require visible vendor attribution unless agreed otherwise. Either negotiate a waiver (Twelve Data white-label tier) or plan a fixed "Data provided by X" line in every tenant terminal, which tenants may dislike.
- Per-tenant agreements: nothing in the API vendors' published terms requires each tenant to sign; exchange regimes (ICE) do require downstream agreements and declarations. Expect a vendor to ask for either (a) a pass-through end-user clause in our tenant contract, or (b) a list of tenants. Option (a) is normal and cheap; we should offer it.

### Gaps
- No API vendor publishes how it prices white label (per tenant, per platform, flat). Twelve Data Enterprise+ is custom.
- No FX venue or FX vendor policy text defining "white label" was found; the definition used here is an equities exchange filing from 2018.

## 3. Derived data: are candles, charts, marked-up spreads and simulated fills derived data, and can we redistribute them freely?

### Takeaway
Mostly no. Every definition found makes derived data depend on not being able to recover or substitute for the original prices, and Tiingo's 2026 terms expressly list resampled or aggregated price data, "adjusting" bid/ask, and charts that display the data as prohibited derived products; so candles, charts, marked-up quotes and fill prices are the licensed data itself and need the display/redistribution licence. Genuinely derived outputs are aggregate statistics such as P&L totals, drawdown and win rate.

### Cited Findings
- Tiingo, section 1.6(c) (verbatim, 2026-08-05): a Derived Product may be distributed without approval "only if the Derived Product at all times satisfies both of the following requirements: (i) it is not, and cannot reasonably be used as, a substitute for access to, purchase of, or use of any Tiingo Data or Service; and (ii) it cannot reasonably be reverse engineered, reconstructed, decoded, disaggregated, matched, combined with other information, or otherwise used to identify, recover, or reproduce any underlying Tiingo Data." - [Tiingo Terms of Use](https://app.tiingo.com/tos/)
- Tiingo prohibited examples (verbatim): "simple transformations of open, high, low, close, volume, bid, ask, trade, quote ... including renaming, reordering, reformatting, rounding, filtering, adjusting, or changing the file format or schema"; "resampled, aggregated, interpolated, normalized, rebased, indexed, currency-converted, unit-converted, or time-zone-shifted data when the output reproduces, closely tracks, or can be used to recover the underlying Tiingo Data"; "tables, files, feeds, APIs, databases, dashboards, charts, downloads, or query tools that display, deliver, or permit extraction of Tiingo Data or a substantially equivalent substitute". Permitted examples include "aggregate or statistical results of a backtest, such as Sharpe or Sortino ratios, alpha, beta, maximum drawdown, win rate, or aggregate profit and loss". "Company may determine in its reasonable discretion whether a Derived Product satisfies this Section." - [Tiingo Terms of Use](https://app.tiingo.com/tos/)
- Twelve Data (summarising fetch, 2026-01-01): "Derived Data": "data created by Customer from the Data, provided such data cannot be reverse-engineered to arrive at the underlying Data"; customers retain rights to compliant derived data (Section 6.2) - [Twelve Data Terms](https://twelvedata.com/terms)
- ICE (verbatim, Jan 2026): Derived Data is data created "as a result of combining, processing, changing, converting or calculating the Exchange's market data ... with other data where the resultant data: (i) does not bear resemblance to the underlying Exchange's market data; (ii) cannot be readily reverse engineered ...; (iii) cannot be used in a manner which could be a source of, or a substitute for Data provided by ICE Data, (iv) cannot be used in whole or in part in a manner which competes with ICE Data or its Affiliates; or (v) cannot be used for constructing, creating or calculating the value of any index or indexed products." Derived data is still licensed and fee-liable under a separate Derived Data License Agreement - [ICE Futures Europe Market Data Policy v4](https://www.ice.com/publicdocs/futures/ICE_Futures_Europe_Market_Data_Policy.pdf)
- Cboe BZX (pre-2025, verbatim): "'Derived Data' is pricing data or other data that (i) is created in whole or in part from Exchange Data, (ii) is not an index or financial product, and (iii) cannot be readily reverse-engineered to recreate Exchange Data or used to create other data that is a reasonable facsimile or substitute for Exchange Data." - [SR-CboeBZX-2018-065, Exhibit 5](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/cboebzx/2018/34-84002-ex5.pdf)
- TraderMade (summarising fetch): users may not "reproduce, copy, modify, distribute, market, display, transfer, sublicense, assign, prepare derivative work(s) or adaptation(s) based on" the API or data - [TraderMade terms](https://marketdata.tradermade.com/termsandconditions)
- Live-Rates (verbatim): display "either as received or derived from it (for example charts, OHLC candles ...)" is allowed on paid plans - [Live-Rates terms](https://www.live-rates.com/terms-privacy)
- Secondary: "A derived metric can be deemed 'redistribution' if it exposes enough granularity to reconstruct the source" - [Elliptic corpus](https://www.elliptic.co/corpus/gen-3835/market-data/market-data-licensing-redistribution-rights-and-compliance-controls.html)

### Inferences
- Candles (OHLC per minute or hour) resample the ticks and "closely track" the source, so they are the vendor's data, not derived data. Charts are a display of the data. Both need the display licence; Live-Rates is the only vendor found that names candles and charts as allowed on standard paid plans.
- Marked-up bid/ask: widening a spread is "adjusting" a bid/ask (Tiingo's prohibited "simple transformation"). It also fails the reverse-engineering test because a fixed or group-known markup can simply be subtracted, and traders see both sides. So marked-up quotes remain the vendor's data. Markup does not reduce licence needs; it adds an attribution question (showing "Data provided by X" next to prices X never quoted) that should be settled in the contract.
- Simulated fills and trade history: each fill price is a (marked-up) quote at a point in time, so trade logs, statements and exports that show entry/exit prices contain vendor data. Showing a trader their own fills is ordinary display; exporting full trade histories to tenants (CSV, API) for their own analytics or payouts is closer to redistribution and should be named in the licence.
- Account-level aggregates (balance, equity curve, P&L, drawdown, win rate, consistency scores) fit Tiingo's permitted examples and the Twelve Data definition, and are the outputs tenants most need. These are the strongest candidates for "derived data we own", which matters for what we may keep after a licence ends (section 4).
- The vendor decides: Tiingo reserves "reasonable discretion" on whether something is a Derived Product. So we should not rely on our own classification; list the outputs in the order form.

### Gaps
- No FX-specific derived data policy (EBS, Cboe FX, LSEG, Bloomberg) was read. A search snippet attributes to a Bloomberg licence the wording that a derived rights licence "only covers internal proprietary use of the data for creating algorithms, price formation, or risk engines, and does not include index creation, financial instrument creation or any other derived rights", but the snippet's source (possibly a 2020 Brazilian CVM procurement contract) could not be confirmed - [search context: CVM contract](https://conteudo.cvm.gov.br/export/sites/cvm/licitacao/contrato/anexos/2020/ct23/1_CT_23_2020.pdf).
- No source discusses markups on vendor quotes directly.

## 4. Storage and history: caching, storing and redisplaying history, and what happens to stored data when a licence ends

### Takeaway
Storage is a separately granted right: Tiingo forbids any persistent storage on starter and trial plans and, on paid plans, requires deletion of all Tiingo data on termination "from every system", explicitly including backups and "systems used for legal, regulatory, or compliance retention"; Twelve Data requires deletion within 30 days and may ask for a certificate. Our 30-day chart history and stored candles are vendor data and would have to be purged when we change vendor, and trade records with fill prices need a written retention carve-out.

### Cited Findings
- Tiingo 1.6(a) (verbatim): on Starter and Trial plans "you may not write, save, archive, back up, or otherwise retain Tiingo Data in any persistent or durable storage", only process it "transiently in volatile memory or in a temporary, non-persistent cache", and the ban covers "local devices, databases, object stores, file systems, logs, queues, archives, backups, and disaster-recovery systems" - [Tiingo Terms of Use](https://app.tiingo.com/tos/)
- Tiingo 1.6(b) (verbatim): on Paid Plans "you may persist Tiingo Data in storage solely to the extent permitted by that Paid Plan and these Terms. Upon the expiration, cancellation, or termination of the Paid Plan ... you must promptly and permanently delete all Tiingo Data from every system ... including production systems, local storage, logs, queues, archives, backups, disaster-recovery systems, and systems used for legal, regulatory, or compliance retention. A different retention period applies only if Company expressly agrees to it in a separate written agreement." And from 1.6(c): "Derived Products that satisfy this Section may be retained after your subscription ends; Tiingo Data may not." - [Tiingo Terms of Use](https://app.tiingo.com/tos/)
- Twelve Data (summarising fetch, 2026-01-01): prohibited to "Store or cache Data beyond permitted timeframes specified in the Documentation"; after termination "All Data must be deleted within 30 days", and certification of deletion may be required - [Twelve Data Terms](https://twelvedata.com/terms)
- TraderMade (summarising fetch): users may not "systematically query our data at intervals with the aim to store, display or distribute that data"; retention after termination is not allowed - [TraderMade terms](https://marketdata.tradermade.com/termsandconditions)
- Live-Rates (verbatim, Oct 2026): paid plans may "Store, cache and aggregate the data you receive, including building your own price history"; the terms say nothing about deletion at termination - [Live-Rates terms](https://www.live-rates.com/terms-privacy)
- ICE prices redistribution as covering "Real Time, Delayed and Historical" (historical is a licensed category in its own right) and sells historical data as separate subscriptions - [ICE Futures Europe Market Data Policy v4](https://www.ice.com/publicdocs/futures/ICE_Futures_Europe_Market_Data_Policy.pdf)
- Record keeping for audits: LSEG's audit schedule requires customers "to maintain the Data usage records at least for the last five (5) years" (pre-2025) - [LSE Market Data Schedule E, v6.0, 1 Jan 2023](https://docs.londonstockexchange.com/sites/default/files/documents/schedule-e-market-data-audit-2023.pdf)

### Inferences
- Our stored ticks, candles and 30-day chart history are "Data" under all three API vendors' terms and need an explicit storage grant (how long, what granularity, whether backups count).
- Switching vendors: under Tiingo or Twelve Data terms we would have to purge vendor A's history, including backups, and rebuild charts from vendor B. The platform already reloads 30 days of chart history from the price feed when the feed is switched (commit 8a120d7 in this repository), which fits this obligation, but backups and logs that contain ticks would also need purging.
- Trade records conflict with deletion clauses: closed trades, statements and breach evidence carry fill prices, and tenants need them for payouts, disputes and their own accounting. Tiingo's clause expressly reaches "systems used for legal, regulatory, or compliance retention", so without a carve-out we could be forced to delete or redact prices in trade records. Negotiate: "trade and account records of end users, including execution prices, may be retained indefinitely for record-keeping, dispute and legal purposes and are not Data subject to deletion".
- Logging: raw ticks in application logs, queues (Redis), and DR snapshots count as storage under Tiingo's wording; keep tick data out of general logs.

### Gaps
- Twelve Data's "permitted timeframes specified in the Documentation" were not located.
- No vendor states how long derived candles may be kept during the subscription.

## 5. End-user counting, reporting and audits; enforcement examples

### Takeaway
OTC FX API vendors on published plans count usage (API calls, WebSocket credits) rather than end users, but reserve audit rights and overage charges; exchange-style regimes count users or devices monthly, audit with long lookbacks (LSEG: five years, customer pays if more than 5% off, plus interest), and charge for late reports (ICE: USD 5,000 after the third missed month). No enforcement case was found against an FX broker or prop firm for spot FX price redistribution; the known cases concern platforms that passed licensed data to more downstream users than licensed (IDC v. Tradeweb, about USD 80 million claimed) or kept using data after a corporate change (Bloomberg v. UBS).

### Cited Findings
- Twelve Data (summarising fetch, 2026-01-01): "Twelve Data may audit Customer's use upon reasonable notice to ensure compliance"; usage-based fees "calculated based on API calls, data volume, or other metrics" and "overage charges apply for exceeding subscription limits"; liability cap "SHALL NOT EXCEED THE FEES PAID IN THE TWELVE MONTHS PRECEDING THE CLAIM"; governed by Singapore law with SIAC arbitration - [Twelve Data Terms](https://twelvedata.com/terms)
- TraderMade (summarising fetch): "In event of a breach of 'Fair Business Use', TraderMade reserves the right to claim reasonable remuneration"; liability limited to fees for the current term; UK law; no explicit audit clause - [TraderMade terms](https://marketdata.tradermade.com/termsandconditions)
- ICE (verbatim, Jan 2026): "ICE Data Services will require that all firms with access to pricing data over impact price servers submit a monthly declaration quantifying all end users. Failure to report monthly access declarations will result in a delinquent reporting fee of $5,000 after the third delinquent month and may result in ICE restricting future access to market data." - [ICE Futures Europe Market Data Policy v4](https://www.ice.com/publicdocs/futures/ICE_Futures_Europe_Market_Data_Policy.pdf)
- LSEG / London Stock Exchange Schedule E (pre-2025, v6.0, 1 Jan 2023, verbatim): "The minimum audit period is five (5) years unless the Customer has been audited in the last 5 years"; audit notice "a minimum of thirty (30) days' notice ... Less than thirty days' notice may be provided (i) where the relevant Exchange reasonably suspects non-compliance"; "Should an audit determine that the Charges paid by the Customer over the period being investigated were more than five percent (5%) inaccurate, the reasonable cost of that investigation shall be paid by the Customer"; underpayment is payable "plus interest ... Interest shall run from the date 30 days after the day on which each relevant Report was due." - [LSE Market Data Schedule E](https://docs.londonstockexchange.com/sites/default/files/documents/schedule-e-market-data-audit-2023.pdf)
- Under-reporting is common (secondary, undated snippet): global firms reportedly report "between 35 and 40 percent of what they're actually using" - [WatersTechnology: Understanding under-reporting](https://www.waterstechnology.com/data-management/1616345/understanding-under-reporting)
- IDC Financial Publishing v. BondDesk, Tradeweb and Fidelity (pre-2025; filed Sept 2015, E.D. Wisconsin): IDC alleged that while some Fidelity units were licensed to receive its data via Tradeweb's platform, the data was "also distributed without authorization to Fidelity's institutional customers for approximately five years", claimed "approximately $80 million in damages" plus punitive damages; some claims were dismissed in 2019 and the rest settled in early 2020 "without any material impact" to Tradeweb - [Tradeweb Form S-1 (2020)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1758730/000110465920048747/tv539139-s1.htm) (from search snippets of Tradeweb's filings)
- Bloomberg v. UBS (pre-2025; settled 30 Sept 2020, terms undisclosed): dispute over 1998 licences and a 2005 addendum letting UBS use Bloomberg data within UBS Delta for third-party clients for USD 300,000 a year; Bloomberg alleged breach of redistribution restrictions after StatPro bought UBS Delta and kept using the data - [A-Team Insight, 14 Oct 2020](https://a-teaminsight.com/?p=43071)
- BGC v. Tullett Prebon: BGC sued for allegedly illegally accessed US Treasuries market data, seeking "hundreds of millions of dollars" (date not confirmed; pre-2025) - [WatersTechnology: BGC sues Tullett](https://waterstechnology.com/node/1896111)
- 2015 exchange clamp-down on unlicensed downstream CFD brokers with threats of backdated fees is already covered in the prior note - [Finance Magnates, Aug 2015](https://financemagnates.com/institutional-forex/execution/exchanges-clamp-down-as-unlicensed-firms-distribute-their-data-feeds)

### Inferences
- With a flat-fee API vendor, the audit risk is not miscounted users but scope: an audit (or simply a look at a tenant's public sign-up page) shows prices under another company's brand, and the vendor argues that "External display" did not include white label. The back-bill would then be the white-label/enterprise price for the whole period, possibly per tenant, plus the cost of an immediate upgrade.
- The IDC case is the closest analogue to us: a platform licensed for some downstream users served more downstream users than licensed, for years. Our equivalent is adding tenants beyond what the licence covers. Keep a tenant register with go-live dates; it is our evidence in any audit.
- Bloomberg v. UBS shows change of control as a trigger: if Ludware or Kronant is sold or a tenant's platform is spun out, licences that are not assignable become a problem. Ask for assignment on change of control.
- Liability caps (12 months' fees, Twelve Data; current term fees, TraderMade) protect the vendor, not us. Our exposure to tenants if the feed stops is set by our own tenant contracts.

### Gaps
- No FX vendor or venue audit schedule (lookback, interest) was found; the five-year LSEG schedule is equities/LSEG and from 2023.
- No public case was found of a vendor or venue suing or back-billing an FX broker, CFD broker or prop firm specifically for spot FX or spot metals price redistribution.

## 6. Precious metals: does dealer spot XAUUSD/XAGUSD need anything beyond the vendor licence, and what about the LBMA Silver Price?

### Takeaway
Dealer or aggregator spot XAUUSD and XAGUSD prices are OTC quotes covered by the vendor licence alone, like FX. The benchmarks are different: both the LBMA Gold Price and the LBMA Silver Price are administered by ICE Benchmark Administration (IBA), with LBMA owning the IP, and anyone using them "for valuation and pricing activities and in transactions and financial products" needs an IBA usage licence; real-time or historical benchmark data for use or redistribution also needs an IBA licence.

### Cited Findings
- LBMA Silver Price page (verbatim): "The LBMA Silver Price is administered independently by ICE Benchmark Administration (IBA)." "IBA independently administers the price and provides the auction platform on which the LBMA Silver Price is calculated, while LBMA own the intellectual property rights." "Any party using the LBMA Silver Price for valuation and pricing activities and in transactions and financial products requires a usage licence with IBA." The page also says (paraphrase by fetch tool) that real-time or historical LBMA Silver Price data for use or redistribution requires a licence from IBA - [LBMA Silver Price](https://www.lbma.org.uk/prices-and-data/lbma-silver-price)
- IBA runs licence-holder consultations on the gold and silver prices (2024 questionnaire, pre-2025) - [IBA LBMA Gold/Silver Prices questionnaire 2024](https://www.ice.com/publicdocs/LBMA_Gold_Silver_Prices_Questionnaire_Licence_Holders_2024.pdf)
- LBMA Gold Price: same IBA licence rule, intraday data delayed by 4 hours (prior note) - [LBMA prices](https://www.lbma.org.uk/prices-and-data/lbma-precious-metal-prices)

### Inferences
- XAUUSD/XAGUSD from a dealer/aggregator feed: no extra licence beyond the vendor's. The two traps are (1) using the LBMA Gold or Silver Price anywhere (for example as a daily reference, end-of-day valuation, settlement of accounts, or in labels), which "valuation and pricing activities" would catch, and (2) a vendor whose "spot" metal price is actually derived from COMEX/CME futures, which could bring CME licensing in. Ask the vendor to confirm in writing that its XAU and XAG prices are OTC dealer spot and not derived from exchange futures or LBMA benchmarks.
- Do not use "LBMA" in instrument names or descriptions; LBMA owns the IP.

### Gaps
- The brief mentions CME as a possible silver administrator. The current LBMA page names only IBA; the history of who administered the silver price before IBA was not verified in this session.
- The IBA fee schedule was not read (not needed unless we use the benchmarks).
- No vendor statement on the source of its XAU/XAG spot price was found.

## 7. EU and Swedish law: database right, contract law, MiFIR "reasonable commercial basis", GDPR

### Takeaway
Contract is the main constraint; statute adds little help and some extra exposure. MiFIR's reasonable-commercial-basis rules (now in Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/1156, applying from 23 August 2026) cover only data that trading venues, systematic internalisers, APAs and CTPs must publish for financial instruments, and spot FX is not a MiFID financial instrument, so we get no regulated price or non-discrimination right for our data. The EU database right may protect a vendor's compiled feed even without a contract, and Sweden's version (49 § upphovsrättslagen) is broader than the Directive because "a large number of data" alone qualifies. GDPR matters only if a vendor wants named end-user reporting.

### Cited Findings
- MiFIR RCB scope (secondary summary): Article 13 MiFIR requires "market operators and investment firms operating a trading venue, approved publication arrangements ('APAs'), consolidated tape providers ('CTPs') and systematic internalisers, to make pre-trade and post-trade information on transactions in financial instruments available to the public on a reasonable commercial basis" - [A&O Shearman FinReg](https://finreg.aoshearman.com/EuropeanSecuritiesandMarkets-Authority-publish)
- ESMA public statement, 7 July 2026 (verbatim): "ESMA recalls that the RTS on RCB applies in full to market data providers within its scope from 23 August 2026." From 23 August to 30 September 2026 NCAs were asked not to prioritise action on "the application of fees and the corresponding issuance of invoices reflecting revised fee schedules", without affecting "non-discriminatory access, fair contractual terms, transparency obligations and the provision of delayed market data" - [ESMA74-276584410-11272](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-07/ESMA74-276584410-11272_RCB_deprioritisation_statement.pdf)
- The RTS is Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/1156 of 12 June 2025; ESMA withdrew its 2021 market data guidelines in February 2026 because the content moved into the RTS (snippets) - [EUR-Lex 2025/1156](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_del/2025/1156/oj/eng); [ESMA BoS decision notice, Feb 2026](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-02/ESMA74-276584410-11228_BoS_Decision_Notice_-_RCB_Guidelines_Withdrawal.pdf)
- ESMA's own definition of "market data" (as quoted in ICE's Jan 2026 policy): "Market Data should mean the data trading venues, SIs, APAs and CTPs have to make public for the purpose of the pre-trade and post-trade transparency regime." - [ICE Futures Europe Market Data Policy v4](https://www.ice.com/publicdocs/futures/ICE_Futures_Europe_Market_Data_Policy.pdf)
- Spot FX is outside MiFID II: "Spot FX is not a 'financial instrument' within the scope of MiFID 2", per Article 10(2) of Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/565, which defines a spot contract as delivery within 2 trading days for major pairs (secondary) - [Jolly Contrarian: FX Spot](https://jollycontrarian.com/index.php/FX_Spot); text of the regulation: [legislation.gov.uk, Reg. 2017/565](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2017/565/2020-01-31); MFSA on FX forwards (pre-2025) - [MFSA MiFID forward contracts](https://www.mfsa.mt/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/20180813_MiFID_FowardContracts.pdf)
- CME publishes a MiFID II market data overview specifically for EBS (pre-2025), consistent with EBS's MiFID-regulated FX business (forwards/NDFs) being in scope while spot is not (title only seen) - [CME EBS MiFID II market data overview](https://cmegroup.com/trading/market-tech-and-data-services/files/mifidii-market-data-overview-ebs-sept-2024.pdf)
- EU database right, CJEU C-203/02 British Horseracing Board v William Hill (pre-2025, 2004, still leading law; secondary summaries): "substantial investment" covers only "seeking, collecting, verifying and presenting existing materials and not the resources used to create the materials which make up the database" - [Pinsent Masons Out-Law](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/guides/british-horseracing-board-v-wi); [CJEU press release](https://curia.europa.eu/jcms/jcms/P_26758/fr)
- Sweden, 49 § upphovsrättslagen (1960:729), katalogskydd: the maker of a catalogue, table or similar work that "compiles a large number of data or is the result of a substantial investment" has an exclusive right to make copies and make it available to the public; protection lasts 15 years from creation or first publication; the latest amendment shown is SFS 2026:1120 - [lagen.nu, URL 49 §](https://lagen.nu/1960:729#P49). Swedish case law summary (pre-2025): protection can follow either from a large number of compiled data or from a substantial investment, and the right protects the compilation, not the individual data - [NJA 2005 s. 924 (lagen.nu)](https://lagen.nu/dom/nja/2005s924)
- Governing law in vendor terms: Twelve Data - Singapore courts and SIAC arbitration - [Twelve Data Terms](https://twelvedata.com/terms); TraderMade - UK law - [TraderMade terms](https://marketdata.tradermade.com/termsandconditions); Live-Rates - German law and courts - [Live-Rates terms](https://www.live-rates.com/terms-privacy)
- GDPR: personal data must be "adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed" (Art. 5(1)(c)) and "collected for specified, explicit and legitimate purposes" (Art. 5(1)(b)) - [GDPR Art. 5](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-5-gdpr/). Transfers to a third country "shall take place only if ... the conditions laid down in this Chapter are complied with by the controller and processor, including for onward transfers" (Art. 44) - [GDPR Art. 44](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-44-gdpr/)

### Inferences
- MiFIR RCB does not apply to the spot FX and spot metals data we buy, whether it comes from an API vendor, an LP or the spot side of a venue. We cannot invoke "reasonable commercial basis", per-user pricing rules or non-discrimination against an FX data vendor. If we later add FX forwards, CFDs priced from venue data or EU equity indices, RCB could apply to that venue data. ESMA's RCB terminology (unit of count, display/non-display) is still a good vocabulary for negotiating.
- Database right: an aggregator (Tiingo, Twelve Data) that collects quotes from many LPs arguably invests in "obtaining" and "verifying", so its compiled feed can be a protected database; a single dealer that creates its own quotes is weaker under BHB. In Sweden, 49 § also protects a work that simply "compiles a large number of data", so a continuous tick stream redistributed in bulk could infringe even if the contract were void. Practical effect: the right adds a non-contractual claim against systematic extraction (for example, a tenant or trader scraping our feed and reselling it), which makes our anti-scraping controls and tenant contract clauses more important. This is an inference; no case on FX feeds was found.
- Contract law: the main vendors pick Singapore, UK or German law. A Swedish company has little chance of relying on Swedish rules on unfair terms against such a contract; negotiate the terms instead. (Inference; Swedish contract law sources not researched.)
- GDPR: tenant traders' identities are personal data, most likely with the tenant as controller and Ludware as processor (inference about our role). If a vendor asks for named end-user reporting, sending names would need a legal basis, the tenant's instruction (DPA), and a Chapter V transfer basis if the vendor is outside the EEA (Twelve Data: Singapore law; Tiingo: US). Data minimisation argues for reporting counts only (ICE asks only for a declaration "quantifying all end users") or pseudonymous IDs. Put "aggregated counts only, no personal data" in the order form.

### Gaps
- The EUR-Lex text of Regulation 2025/1156 did not render; scope wording is from secondary sources and ESMA's statement.
- No CJEU or Swedish case on financial price feeds under the database right was found; CV-Online Latvia (C-762/19, 2021) was not checked.
- Swedish contract law (avtalslagen) and bookkeeping retention rules were not researched.

## 8. Prop firm angle: do regulators require simulated prices from licensed real feeds, or disclosure of the price source?

### Takeaway
No regulator statement was found that requires simulated prop prices to come from a licensed real feed or that requires disclosure of the price source. Regulators focus on making clear that trading is simulated and on fair treatment of traders; the one enforcement case that touched prices (CFTC v. My Forex Funds, 2023) alleged execution "at worse prices than appeared to the customer", and was dismissed in 2025 with sanctions against the CFTC. Licensing risk for us is therefore contractual, while price integrity and disclosure of markups is a reputational and dispute risk for tenants.

### Cited Findings
- CFTC v. Traders Global Group (My Forex Funds), release of 1 Sept 2023 (pre-2025): alleged use of "specialized software to cause customer orders to be executed at worse prices than appeared to the customer at the time an order was sent", "misleadingly assessing commissions that reduce customer account equity", and "pretexts to terminate customer accounts" - [Mondo Visione reprint of CFTC release](https://mondovisione.com/media-and-resources/news/cftc-charges-my-forex-funds-with-fraudulently-taking-over-300-million-from-cu-202391/). A federal judge dismissed the case in May 2025, finding the CFTC acted in bad faith, and ordered it to pay over USD 3.1 million in costs (secondary, search snippets) - [WikiFX, Sept 2025](https://www.wikifx.com/en/newsdetail/202509166484694051.html); [Finance Magnates via TradingView](https://vn.tradingview.com/news/financemagnates:035c61ef0094b:0-dissecting-my-forex-funds-model-how-did-the-prop-trading-firm-generate-310m)
- Consob (Italy, July 2024, pre-2025): warned about prop firms as exercises that "simulate online trading activity in a finance video game" (secondary) - [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/another-regulator-warns-against-prop-firms-calls-them-trading-video-games)
- Belgium (2024, pre-2025): retail prop-trading warning about challenge firms using demo trading without authorisation (secondary snippet, primary not read) - [NexusFi prop firm regulatory status](https://nexusfi.com/a/prop-firms/prop-firm-regulatory-status)
- Industry practice: FTMO says "we emulate the conditions of a real market" (page modified 2026-10-04) - [FTMO FAQ](https://ftmo.oanda.com/faq/what-is-trading-according-to-a-real-market/); a search snippet of FTMO's infrastructure FAQ says it uses "real-time market data feeds from active market maker participants" and that "all accounts FTMO provides to clients are demo accounts with fictitious funds" - [FTMO infrastructure FAQ](https://ftmo.oanda.com/faq/how-does-the-ftmo-us-technical-infrastructure-work/)
- Unverified, do not rely: a marketing blog lists Q3 2026 items (SEC actions on 15 Aug 2026 against two prop firms for "misrepresenting simulated trading as live trading", NFA Notice I-26-12 on 30 Aug 2026, FCA guidance on 5 Sep 2026, an ESMA "joint statement" on 10 Sep 2026, ASIC guidance on 15 Sep 2026) and itself notes that "Price feeds and data-source disclosure are notably absent". It links only to regulators' generic news pages; my searches for NFA I-26-12 and an ESMA prop firm statement found no primary source - [Track360 Q3 2026 roundup](https://track360.io/blog/prop-firm-regulation-news-roundup-q3-2026)

### Inferences
- There is no regulatory rule that our prices must come from a licensed feed; the licence matters because of the vendor contract and database right, not because of prop regulation.
- The live regulatory theme is "simulated must be clearly simulated" plus fair execution. Because we widen bid/ask per group, tenants should disclose that prices are indicative, derived from a third-party feed, and include the firm's markup; we should give tenants a standard disclosure text and keep tick-level records (source quote, markup, fill) so a tenant can prove a disputed fill. This also answers the My Forex Funds-type allegation ("worse prices than appeared").
- Naming the vendor to traders is not required by any rule found, but may be required by the vendor's attribution clause (section 2) unless waived.

### Gaps
- No primary FCA, ESMA, CFTC or FI document on price sources for simulated prop accounts was found.
- The Q3 2026 regulatory items in the Track360 blog could not be verified.

## 9. Synthesis: checklist of terms to get in writing, and realistic risks

### Takeaway
Get one order form or side letter that names the exact use case (hosted, multi-tenant, tenant-branded web terminal plus simulated execution) and turns each pitfall above into an express grant: external display to end users of our customers, white label without vendor attribution, non-display use in the engine, candles/charts/marked-up quotes/fill prices, storage and a retention carve-out for trade records, no per-user or per-tenant fees, and capped audits. The most likely real risk is scope mismatch (buying "external display" and being told white label was not included), followed by vendor termination that blacks out every tenant at once and forces deletion of history.

### Cited Findings
- The relevant clause types in current terms: no sublicensing "except as expressly permitted" and white label "Subject to separate agreement" (Twelve Data) - [Twelve Data Terms](https://twelvedata.com/terms); attribution required unless waived and loss of access for failing to attribute - [Twelve Data attribution](https://support.twelvedata.com/en/articles/12647398-attribution-guidelines-for-using-twelve-data); redistribution only "upon special request and permission, and comes with additional fees", mandatory "Data sourced by Tiingo", deletion from backups and compliance systems, vendor discretion on derived products - [Tiingo Terms](https://app.tiingo.com/tos/); "your own end users" vs no relay "as a data feed or API" - [Live-Rates terms](https://www.live-rates.com/terms-privacy); "reasonable remuneration" for breach - [TraderMade terms](https://marketdata.tradermade.com/termsandconditions); five-year audit lookback, 5% cost threshold, interest - [LSE Schedule E (2023)](https://docs.londonstockexchange.com/sites/default/files/documents/schedule-e-market-data-audit-2023.pdf); per-white-label-platform fees - [Cboe 2018 filing](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/cboebzx/2018/34-84002-ex5.pdf); USD 5,000 late-declaration fee - [ICE 2026](https://www.ice.com/publicdocs/futures/ICE_Futures_Europe_Market_Data_Policy.pdf)

### Inferences
Checklist of terms to get in writing from a vendor (each item maps to a finding above):
1. Use case named in the licence: "hosted multi-tenant web trading terminal operated by Ludware, displayed to end users of Ludware's customers (prop firms) under the customers' brands; customers receive no data feed or API".
2. Definition of End User / Subscriber that expressly includes end users of our customers' branded instances (answers Live-Rates' "your own end users", Twelve Data's "Authorized User", "third parties").
3. White label: right to display without vendor branding, or an agreed attribution form (for example one line in the terminal footer or terms page) that tenants accept; waiver of Tiingo-style mandatory "Data sourced by" wording.
4. No per-tenant agreement or registration with the vendor; at most a pass-through end-user clause in our tenant contract (offer this).
5. Fees: flat fee; no per-end-user, per-tenant, per-white-label-platform or per-device fee; state the thresholds that would trigger repricing (tenants, concurrent users, connections, bandwidth, symbols) and the overage price.
6. Non-display: permission to use the data in a simulated execution and matching engine, position valuation, P&L, margin and rule/risk engines operated for end users, with no separate non-display fee and no professional/non-professional classification of end users.
7. Derived and modified data: right to create and display candles (all timeframes), charts, indicators, widened/marked-up bid and ask, fill prices, trade histories, statements and account statistics; how to attribute modified prices; confirmation that account-level statistics are our derived data.
8. Tenant access: tenants' admin users may view their traders' trades, statements and reports containing prices, and export them for their own record-keeping and payouts (not as a feed).
9. Storage: right to store ticks and candles for at least the chart window (30 days) plus backups and DR; no tick data required to be purged from logs within a short window.
10. Retention carve-out: end-user trade and account records with execution prices may be kept after termination for record-keeping, dispute, tax and legal purposes, and are not "Data" subject to deletion; a realistic purge period (for example 90 days) for the rest, covering backups.
11. Instruments and sources: list the 12 symbols; vendor confirms XAUUSD and XAGUSD are OTC dealer/aggregator spot, not derived from COMEX/CME futures or LBMA benchmarks; vendor warrants it has all upstream rights (LPs, venues) to grant the redistribution, and indemnifies us for upstream claims.
12. Audits: at most once a year, 30 days' notice, lookback capped (for example 12-24 months), at vendor's cost unless underpayment exceeds 5-10%, back-billing at the contracted rate without penalty multiples, and a cure period before any suspension.
13. Reporting: aggregated counts only; no personal data of end users; if any personal data is ever needed, a DPA and transfer mechanism.
14. Termination and suspension: notice period (for example 90 days) for vendor convenience termination; no suspension without notice except for clear material breach; a transition period after termination to move to another vendor.
15. Change of control and assignment: licence survives a sale of Ludware or of the product (lesson from Bloomberg v. UBS).
16. Price changes: notice period and cap on annual increases; right to terminate on increase.
17. Service level and liability: uptime, latency, incident notice; liability cap that at least covers fees paid, with IP infringement indemnity outside the cap.
18. Our own tenant contract (flow-down): tenants and their traders may not scrape, relay, resell or embed prices elsewhere; tenants accept vendor attribution if required; tenants disclose that prices are indicative and include their markup.

Realistic risks, most likely first:
1. Scope mismatch (high likelihood if not negotiated): an "external display" tier used for white label. Consequence: back-billing at white-label/enterprise rates for the full period (possibly per tenant), forced upgrade, and leverage for the vendor at renewal.
2. Vendor termination or suspension (medium): one vendor feeds all tenants, so a dispute, an attribution lapse ("may lead to suspension or termination") or the vendor exiting the market stops every tenant's terminal at once, and deletion clauses wipe chart history. Mitigation: second vendor ready, contractual notice and transition.
3. Derived data misclassification (medium): treating candles, charts or marked-up quotes as "our derived data" and keeping or showing them outside the licence. Mitigation: list them in the order form.
4. Leakage through our own API (medium): if our WebSocket is easy to consume, someone can relay it as a free feed, which is exactly what vendors forbid and what the Swedish database right also covers. Mitigation: per-trader authentication, rate limits, no public docs, contract terms with tenants.
5. Retention conflict (medium): deletion clauses that reach compliance systems clash with keeping trade records. Mitigation: carve-out.
6. Audit and back-billing cost (low to medium with flat-fee API vendors; higher with venue or exchange data, where five-year lookbacks, interest and audit costs apply).
7. Benchmark misuse (low if avoided): any use of the LBMA Gold or Silver Price needs an IBA licence.
8. GDPR (low): only if a vendor insists on named end-user reporting.
9. Regulatory (low for licensing; medium for price integrity disputes handled by tenants).

### Gaps
- No vendor has confirmed any of the checklist items in writing; the published terms leave items 2-8 and 10 open for every vendor reviewed.
- Bloomberg, LSEG and Cboe FX contract terms were not available publicly, so the checklist is built mainly from API vendor terms and exchange policies.
